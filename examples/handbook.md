# Example Lab Handbook

Version 2026-01-10. This handbook is fictional. It shows the kind of document the onboarding bot answers from;
replace it with your own lab's handbook (a Word file, a Google Doc link in DOC_URL, or a Markdown/text file).

## 1. Welcome

Welcome to the Example Lab. We study [your research area]. This handbook covers the
practical things a new member needs in the first weeks: getting access, working safely, buying things, travelling and
taking time off. Ask the PI or the lab manager when something here is unclear.

## 2. First week checklist

- Get your university ID card at the campus card office (bring a photo ID).
- Ask the lab manager to add you to the lab Slack, the shared drive and the lab calendar.
- Complete the online lab safety training and send the certificate to the lab manager.
- Request building access through the facilities portal; the lab manager approves it.
- Read sections 3 to 7 of this handbook.

## 3. Lab safety

- No food or drink in the wet lab. Use the break room on the second floor.
- Lab coats and safety glasses are required at every wet bench. Spare coats are in the cabinet by the door.
- Chemical waste goes in the labelled containers under the fume hood; never down the sink.
- Report every injury or spill to the lab manager on the same day, even small ones.

## 4. Working hours and communication

- Core hours are 10:00 to 16:00 on weekdays. Outside core hours, tell the lab channel when you work alone in the wet
  lab.
- Group meeting is every Tuesday at 13:00 in Room 210. Each member presents about twice a semester.
- Use Slack for lab communication; use email for anything that involves people outside the lab.

## 5. Purchasing (overview)

The lab buys supplies with a purchasing card (P-card) held by the lab manager. Send purchase requests in the
#lab-orders channel with a link, the quantity and the project. Keep every receipt: the purchasing notes have the
current rules, which replace this section where they differ.

## 6. Travel (overview)

Conference travel needs the PI's approval before you book anything. Book flights through the university travel
portal. Keep all receipts; the travel notes describe the current reimbursement steps.

## 7. Time off

Full-time members get 15 working days of vacation per year, plus university holidays. Put planned time off on the lab
calendar at least two weeks ahead and tell the PI. For sick days, send a short message to the PI and the lab manager
on the same morning.
